01

Spray Atomization CFD, Effervescent Atomizer

FlagshipMultiphase
ENGR 7961 Industrial Stage · Concordia University · ANSYS Fluent 2025 R2 · Jan to Apr 2026 · two person team with Kelechi Nwokoagbara
≈100 µmSauter mean diameter (D32)
8Progressive simulation trials
9.5MParticles tracked in 2,012 parcels

Problem

An effervescent atomizer needed a two-phase CFD framework, built first with water and air, that a later gel propellant study could build on.

Approach

Our first Volume of Fluid model captured the mixing inside the injector well, but it never broke the liquid into droplets. We diagnosed why and moved to a coupled VOF to DPM model on a 32 orifice geometry, using sphere of influence refinement at the nozzle exit to keep the mesh at 540,985 elements, inside the student license limit.

Validation

A 2D trial on a 22,500 cell domain confirmed the breakup behavior before we committed to full 3D. Switching pressure velocity coupling from SIMPLE to PISO and tuning under relaxation to 0.3 for pressure and 0.5 for momentum brought every residual below 10-4.

Result

The final model settled on a D32 of 9.98 × 10-5 m, with droplets from 1.56 µm to 705 µm, and it is now the baseline for Newtonian and non Newtonian propellant studies.

02

Numerical Analysis of a VKI Turbine Blade

TurbomachineryPropulsion
MECH 6171 Turbomachinery and Propulsion · Concordia University · ANSYS CFX · Sept to Dec 2025
2.14%Total pressure loss, 6,176 Pa
1.065Peak isentropic Mach at 86% chord
219,807Elements after the mesh study

Problem

Model the VKI LS89 turbine cascade, a standard validation case with published pressure and Mach data from the Von Karman Institute, and predict its isentropic Mach distribution and total pressure loss.

Approach

I built the cascade from imported coordinates, extruded it into a 3D domain, meshed it with inflation layers at the blade surface, and ran k-epsilon turbulence with periodic boundaries to represent an infinite blade row, at 415 K total temperature and 288 kPa total pressure at the inlet.

Validation

A mesh independence study across several element sizes settled on a 2 mm mesh, past which the pressure barely moved. Residuals were driven to 10-12 over 3,000 iterations while I tracked outlet mass flow, and the results were checked against the benchmark test data.

Result

Total pressure loss came out at 6,176 Pa, about 2.14% of inlet total pressure, and the isentropic Mach number climbed from 0.562 at the leading edge to 1.065 at 86% chord. Low pressure on the suction side and high pressure on the pressure side is exactly how a working turbine blade should behave.

03

Design of an Aircraft Winglet

External aerodynamics
MECH 6451 Computer Aided Mechanical Design · Concordia University · CATIA V5, ANSYS Fluent · Sept to Dec 2024
28%Lower drag coefficient than the plain wing
+1.1%Higher lift coefficient
M 0.85Cruise at 35,000 ft

Problem

Design a winglet for an Airbus A350 style wing that cuts induced drag, with cant angle as the main design variable across 30° to 90°.

Approach

I modeled the wing and winglet in CATIA V5, estimating chord lengths and airfoil sections from published A350 dimensions and NASA supercritical airfoil data where exact figures were not public, then ran both configurations in ANSYS Fluent over 500 solver iterations.

Validation

The wing with and without the winglet ran under identical cruise conditions and setup, so the difference comes from the geometry alone, and the final span was checked against ICAO Aerodrome Reference Code limits for commercial aircraft.

Result

The winglet gave a 28% lower drag coefficient and a 1.1% higher lift coefficient than the plain wing. The streamlines show why: the plain tip lets high pressure air spill around it unchecked, while the winglet turns that same spill upward along its surface.

04 · Compressible flow

Flow Behaviour in a Converging Diverging Nozzle

MECH 6111 Gas Dynamics · Concordia University · ANSYS Fluent, density based solver · Sept to Dec 2025

I took a single planar nozzle, sized from isentropic flow theory for an inlet Mach number of 0.5, and pushed it through five regimes by sweeping the outlet back pressure: subsonic choked flow, isentropic supersonic design flow, a normal shock inside the diverging section, an oblique shock outside the nozzle, and expansion waves.

Every case ran on a structured quadrilateral mesh with an inviscid ideal gas model, and I checked each one against one dimensional isentropic flow theory and normal shock tables before reading the contours. The results agreed within 2.2%.

  • Raising outlet pressure to 165 kPa pushed the normal shock into the diverging section, where Mach drops from supersonic to subsonic across a few millimeters.
  • Lowering it to 50 kPa moved the shock outside the nozzle and weakened it into an oblique shock.
  • The subsonic choked case showed flow recirculation.
05 · Low speed aerodynamics

Aerodynamic Optimization of a UAV

MECH 6121 Aerodynamics · Concordia University · XFoil, OpenVSP · Jan to Apr 2025 · team project with Tchokote N. Ulrich

We compared six NACA profiles (2412, 2410, 4415, 4412, 6412 and 6409) in XFoil for pressure coefficient, lift, drag and pitching moment from -5° to 15° angle of attack, then built the strongest pairs into two conceptual UAVs in OpenVSP, changing sweep, twist and taper while holding the fuselage and tail fixed.

A three level mesh study showed the coarse mesh under-predicting drag past stall, while the medium and fine meshes landed on nearly the same curve, so the rest of the study ran on the cheaper medium mesh.

  • NACA 6412 and 6409 gave the highest Cl/Cd, but also the most negative pitching moment, so they would need more stabilizer authority to trim.
  • NACA 2410 and 2412 held the flattest, most stable moment curve.
  • NACA 4415 and 4412 were the balanced choice for cruise.

Aircraft Performance Analysis, Robin DR400

MECH 6241 Aircraft Performance · Concordia University · MATLAB · Jan to Apr 2026

We built a MATLAB model from the flight manual's aerodynamic and powerplant data that computes takeoff, climb, cruise, range, endurance and service ceiling for the Robin DR400. Its takeoff ground run came out at 293 m against the 295 m in the manual, a 0.7% difference.

  • Benchmarked the DR400 against the Cessna 172R and Robin CAP10 across seven performance metrics, and found its flap system gives a 33% shorter landing ground run than the Cessna.
08 · Flight control

NASA AD1 Oblique Wing Flight Control Research

MECH 6091 Flight Control Systems · Concordia University · MATLAB and Simulink · Sept to Dec 2025

We modeled the nonlinear six degree of freedom flight dynamics of NASA's AD1 oblique wing research aircraft at 0° and 30° wing sweep, using aerodynamic derivatives estimated from published NASA flight test data.

  • Trim and linearized stability analysis showed a roll response of 1.83 to 2.12 seconds, slower than the 1.4 second Level 1 handling quality requirement for the aircraft's class.
  • A PID roll rate controller, designed and tuned in Simulink against the linear state space models, brought roll response inside 1.4 seconds at both sweep angles.
09 · Navigation

Aircraft Navigation Estimation Using INS and GPS Sensors

ENGR 6461 Avionics Navigation Systems · Concordia University · MATLAB · Dec 2024

Inertial navigation drifts because accelerometer and gyroscope noise compounds through double integration, while GPS is steadier but refreshes slowly. I designed a Recursive Least Squares algorithm in MATLAB, with a forgetting factor and its own gain vector and covariance updates, to fuse the two during a 100 waypoint, 100 second landing descent.

  • RMSE against the true path: 435.2 for INS only, 169.8 for the fused RLS estimate, a 61% cut.
  • GPS alone scored 7.6 at the noise levels I modeled, so the value of the fusion is a single estimate that keeps working when GPS drops out.
